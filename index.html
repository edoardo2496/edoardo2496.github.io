About

A consultant who writes the code himself.

I don't coordinate developers. I don't hand specs to a data team. I sit with leadership, understand the strategic problem, then go design and build the technical solution from scratch — pipeline, model, dashboard, report.

My background is deliberately unconventional: a Master's in Philosophy (110L cum laude) followed by Industrial Management Engineering currently in progress. Philosophy trained me in rigorous argument and systems thinking. Engineering gave me the quantitative grammar. Data science is where both converge into something useful.

Over four years embedded in a multinational industrial environment, I worked across two business units of 450+ people each — building forecasting models, redesigning reporting pipelines, engineering processes, and putting predictive models into production. All of it end to end, without a team to delegate to.

Today I operate as an independent consultant, designing and delivering data and process solutions for SMEs — from lightweight web tools that digitise field operations, to analytical architectures that turn raw operational data into management accounts.

01
Business problem → Workforce planning
ODE-based Operational Flow Model

Management needed to understand whether current staffing levels were truly necessary. I modelled the department's operational flows as a system of ordinary differential equations — mapping workload dynamics, inter-task dependencies, and capacity constraints over time. The model, currently in production, demonstrated that a 14% FTE reduction was achievable while maintaining existing output volumes, enabling targeted redeployment rather than cuts.

Business problem → Data infrastructure
Multi-Source ETL Pipeline & Unified Revenue Layer

Revenue data was siloed across three heterogeneous ERP systems — OLAP cubes (DMX) and relational databases — making consolidated reporting impossible. I designed and implemented an end-to-end ETL pipeline: extraction via Python connectors, transformation and harmonisation including transcoding and business logic, and consolidation into a centralised PostgreSQL data layer. This was the company's first unified revenue dataset, built from zero, and became the foundation for all directional BI reporting.

05
Client: Edilcrema snc · Business problem → Field operations digitisation
SIS — Purchase Order Management Web App

Site managers were tracking purchase orders on paper or informal messages, making cost control per project impossible. I designed and built a zero-dependency web app — deployed on GitHub Pages — that lets field staff log orders with supplier, site, material, quantity, and unit price. Data flows via Google Apps Script into Google Sheets, then into a local SQLite database via a scheduled Python sync, feeding job-level P&L and management accounts. Suppliers and sites load dynamically; order status is tracked end to end (Sent → Confirmed → Received → Invoiced).

06
Client: Edilcrema snc · Business problem → Labour costing
SIS — Site Labour Hours Tracking App

Labour costs were invisible at the job level — no structured way for site foremen to report hours by project, work type, or worker. I built a mobile-first web app designed for use on-site: quick-entry buttons for common hour increments, ordinary vs overtime split, work category classification (masonry, excavation, carpentry, finishes, etc.). Data feeds the same Google Sheets → SQLite pipeline, enabling real unit cost per work type, actual vs budgeted labour variance, and a complete job-level cost account — built from field data collected by the people doing the work.
